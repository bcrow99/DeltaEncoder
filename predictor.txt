PREDICTOR TABLE
==============================================================================

This file describes the predictors a block map (delta type 13) can use: the
primitives, the table of 225 predictors built from them
(DeltaMapper.PREDICTOR_TABLE, delta_mapper.TABLE), how often each was chosen,
and the 32 entries of the old 256-entry table that were dropped. It replaces
earlier versions of this file.

NOTATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pixels are predicted in raster order, so only pixels above and to the left are
known. Around the pixel x being predicted:

              column:  x-2   x-1    x    x+1   x+2
              row y-2:         NNW   bb    NNE
              row y-1:   NWW    c     b     d    NEE
              row y:     aa     a     x

So a is the left neighbour, b the one above, c above-left, d above-right; aa
is two to the left and bb two above; NEE is two right and one up, NNE one
right and two up (LS uses both); NWW and NNW, their mirror images, are not
used by any primitive. R is the previous channel in coding order at the same
pixel, and Ra, Rb, Rc, Rd are its neighbours. Channels are coded in order, so
R is already known when a channel is decoded; on the first channel the cross-
channel primitives fall back to their base predictor. >> rounds down; / is
integer division.

A predictor in the table is either a primitive alone or the average of two,
(P_i + P_j + 1) >> 1. Averaging two predictors whose errors tend to have
opposite signs cancels much of both errors, which is why the table is mostly
averages. Row 0, column 0 and the last column are not predicted by the block
map: row 0 is coded horizontally, column 0 vertically and the last column
horizontally.

HOW A BLOCK MAP USES THE TABLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each channel's interior is split into square blocks (4, 6, 8, 12 or 16, chosen
per channel). The channel carries its own set of up to 255 predictor ids from
the table (one byte each in the map's header), and each block an index into
that set. The survey (DeltaMapper.surveyChannels) builds the set: it estimates
every table entry's coded bits on every block, adds entries in greedy order by
the bits they save net of the map cost, measures the real coded size of the
first n entries for n = 1, 2, 3, ... and keeps the best. The map's entries are
entropy-coded, so their cost depends on how many predictors a channel uses and
how unevenly, not on the size of the table.

PRIMITIVES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMITIVES ON A, B, C, D

   0  a                           in  21 table entries, ~3.92% of pixels
      The pixel to the left. Exact wherever the image is constant along rows:
      horizontal edges, horizontal stripes, flat areas. On its own it is the
      classic 'previous pixel' delta.

   1  b                           in  15 table entries, ~0.85% of pixels
      The pixel above. Exact wherever the image is constant down columns:
      vertical edges and stripes.

   2  c                           in   7 table entries, ~0.24% of pixels
      The pixel above and to the left: follows edges running down-right at 45
      degrees (constant along the NW-SE diagonal).

   3  d                           in  11 table entries, ~0.28% of pixels
      The pixel above and to the right: follows edges running down-left at 45
      degrees (constant along the NE-SW diagonal).

   4  (a+b)/2                     in  19 table entries, ~2.34% of pixels
      = (a + b) >> 1
      The mean of left and above, rounded down. A smooth, low-noise guess for
      gently varying areas; it halves the noise of either neighbour but blurs
      edges.

   5  (b+c)/2                     in  17 table entries, ~0.84% of pixels
      = (b + c) >> 1
      The mean of above and above-left: the value half a pixel to the left on
      the row above, i.e. a direction between vertical and the 45-degree NW
      diagonal (about 63 degrees).

   6  (a+c)/2                     in  20 table entries, ~1.15% of pixels
      = (a + c) >> 1
      The mean of left and above-left: the value half a pixel up in the left
      column, a direction between horizontal and the NW diagonal (about 27
      degrees).

   7  (b+d)/2                     in  19 table entries, ~1.81% of pixels
      = (b + d) >> 1
      The mean of above and above-right: the row above, half a pixel to the
      right; a direction between vertical and the NE diagonal.

   8  (c+d)/2                     in  12 table entries, ~1.09% of pixels
      = (c + d) >> 1
      The mean of the two diagonal neighbours above: the row above at the
      current column, smoothed (a softened b).

   9  (a+b+c+d)/4                 in   9 table entries, ~1.73% of pixels
      = (a + b + c + d + 2) >> 2
      The rounded mean of all four causal neighbours: the smoothest of the
      simple predictors, good in noisy flat areas.

  10  a+b-c                       in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = a + b - c
      The planar (gradient) predictor: fits a plane through a, b and c and
      extends it, so it is exact on linear ramps in any direction. It
      overshoots at edges, which MED (11) and clamp(a+b-c) (40) correct.

  11  MED                         in  10 table entries, ~3.32% of pixels
      The median edge detector of JPEG-LS / LOCO-I: min(a, b) if c >= max(a,
      b), max(a, b) if c <= min(a, b), otherwise a + b - c. It picks a or b at
      a horizontal or vertical edge and the planar predictor in between.

  12  (3a+b)/4                    in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = (3a + b + 2) >> 2
      Three quarters left, one quarter above: mostly horizontal, slightly
      smoothed.

  13  (a+3b)/4                    in   5 table entries, ~0.13% of pixels
      = (a + 3b + 2) >> 2
      One quarter left, three quarters above: mostly vertical, slightly
      smoothed.

  14  (3a+d)/4                    in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = (3a + d + 2) >> 2
      Three quarters left, one quarter above-right.

  15  (3b+a)/4                    in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = (3b + a + 2) >> 2
      Three quarters above, one quarter left. This is exactly the same formula
      as 13; both are kept so that the numbering of later primitives (and old
      files) stays as it was.

  16  (a+d)/2                     in  24 table entries, ~3.28% of pixels
      = (a + d) >> 1
      The mean of left and above-right: the two neighbours on either side of
      the pixel along the NE-SW diagonal, so it interpolates across that
      diagonal; strong on edges running at 45 degrees up-right.

  17  a+(b-c)/2                   in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = a + ((b - c) >> 1)
      Left plus half the horizontal gradient of the row above: a damped planar
      predictor along rows.

  18  b+(a-c)/2                   in  14 table entries, ~0.57% of pixels
      = b + ((a - c) >> 1)
      Above plus half the vertical gradient of the left column: a damped
      planar predictor down columns.

  19  a+d-b                       in   5 table entries, ~0.19% of pixels
      = a + d - b
      The planar predictor along the other diagonal: left plus the gradient
      from b to d. Exact on ramps, like 10, but built from the up-right
      neighbours.

  20  (a+b+d)/3                   in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = (a + b + d + 1) / 3
      The mean of left, above and above-right (integer division).

  21  b+(d-c)/2                   in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = b + ((d - c) >> 1)
      Above plus half the slope of the row above: the row above extended by
      half a step.

SELECTION

  22  Paeth                       in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      PNG's Paeth predictor: forms p = a + b - c and returns whichever of a,
      b, c is closest to p (ties to a, then b). A selection predictor: it
      always returns an actual neighbour.

  23  median(a,b,c)               in  11 table entries, ~1.20% of pixels
      = median(a, b, c)
      The median of left, above and above-left: a robust choice that ignores
      one outlier among the three.

  24  median(a,b,d)               in  12 table entries, ~3.35% of pixels
      = median(a, b, d)
      The median of left, above and above-right.

  25  median(a,d,a+d-b)           in  10 table entries, ~0.64% of pixels
      = median(a, d, a + d - b)
      MED's idea along the up-right diagonal: the median of a, d and their
      planar extension a + d - b.

  26  switch(a,b)                 in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = switch(a, b)
      Whichever of a or b lies in the flatter direction: a if |b - c| <= |a -
      c| (the row above is flat), else b.

  27  switch(a,b,d)               in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = switch(a, b, d)
      The flattest of three directions: a, b or d by the smallest of |b - c|,
      |a - c| and |a - b|.

CROSS-CHANNEL

  28  a+(R-Ra)                    in  13 table entries, ~1.88% of pixels
      = a + (R - Ra)
      Cross-channel: the left pixel plus how the previous channel (R, coded
      first) changed from its own left pixel to this pixel. Copies the other
      channel's local detail onto this one; on the first channel it is just a.

  29  b+(R-Rb)                    in  14 table entries, ~1.20% of pixels
      = b + (R - Rb)
      Cross-channel: above plus the previous channel's change from above.

  30  d+(R-Rd)                    in  11 table entries, ~0.70% of pixels
      = d + (R - Rd)
      Cross-channel: above-right plus the previous channel's change from
      above-right.

  31  MED+(R-MED_R)               in   7 table entries, ~1.14% of pixels
      = MED + (R - MED_R)
      Cross-channel: this channel's MED prediction plus the previous channel's
      MED error at the same pixel, so the error the other channel made is
      assumed here too.

  32  (a+b)/2+(R-..)              in  11 table entries, ~0.47% of pixels
      = (a+b)/2 + (R - (Ra+Rb)/2)
      Cross-channel: the mean of left and above, corrected by the previous
      channel's error for the same predictor.

  33  a+b-c+(R-..)                in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = a + b - c + (R - (Ra + Rb - Rc))
      Cross-channel: the planar predictor corrected by the previous channel's
      planar error.

  34  MED+(R-MED_R)/2             in  18 table entries, ~3.54% of pixels
      = MED + (R - MED_R) / 2
      As 31 with half the correction: for channels that follow the previous
      one only partly.

  35  (a+b)/2+(R-..)/2            in  19 table entries, ~2.12% of pixels
      = (a+b)/2 + (R - ...)/2
      As 32 with half the correction.

LONGER REACH

  36  2a-aa                       in   7 table entries, ~0.34% of pixels
      = 2a - aa
      Linear extrapolation along the row from the two pixels to the left (aa
      is two to the left): continues a horizontal ramp.

  37  2b-bb                       in   9 table entries, ~0.24% of pixels
      = 2b - bb
      Linear extrapolation down the column from the two pixels above (bb is
      two above).

  38  GAP                         in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      CALIC's gradient-adjusted predictor: measures horizontal and vertical
      gradients from seven neighbours (a, aa, b, bb, c, d and the pixel above
      d), picks a or b at a strong edge, otherwise (2(a + b) + d - c) / 4
      nudged toward a or b by how strong the edge is. Thresholds 80, 32 and 8
      (for 8-bit values).

  39  GAP/8                       in  29 table entries, ~7.81% of pixels
      GAP with thresholds an eighth as large (10, 4, 1), for quantized images
      whose values span a smaller range. The second most used primitive after
      LS.

CLAMPED

  40  clamp(a+b-c)                in  14 table entries, ~4.62% of pixels
      = clamp(a + b - c)
      The planar predictor clamped to the range of a, b, c and d: keeps
      exactness on ramps but cannot overshoot at edges.

  41  clamp(a+d-b)                in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = clamp(a + d - b)
      The diagonal planar predictor (19), clamped the same way.

BIAS-CORRECTED

  42  MED+bias                    in  17 table entries, ~6.53% of pixels
      = MED + bias
      MED plus its mean error so far in the pixel's gradient context, as JPEG-
      LS corrects bias: the context is the JPEG-LS quantized gradients of d -
      b, b - c and c - a (9 levels each, 729 contexts); each context keeps the
      sum and count of MED's errors over every earlier pixel in raster order
      (halved when the count reaches 64), and the correction is their rounded
      mean.

  43  (a+b)/2+bias                in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = (a+b)/2 + bias
      The mean of a and b, with the same kind of bias correction.

  44  a+bias                      in   0 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      = a + bias
      The left pixel, bias-corrected.

  45  b+bias                      in   9 table entries, ~0.66% of pixels
      = b + bias
      The pixel above, bias-corrected.

LEAST SQUARES

  46  LS                          in   1 table entries, ~41.84% of pixels
      Least squares: at every pixel, the linear predictor (8 neighbours a, b,
      c, d, aa, bb, the pixel two right one up and the pixel one right two up,
      plus a constant; after the first channel also the previous channel at
      the pixel, to its left and above) that best fits the causal window of
      the 10 rows above (10 columns either side) and the 10 pixels to the
      left, refitted pixel by pixel from exact integer running sums (ridge 1,
      L D L^T in a fixed order, so encoder, decoder and the Python port
      agree). The prediction is clamped to 2 beyond the range of a, b, c and
      d; a until the window has twice as many pixels as unknowns. The single
      most used predictor: about 42% of all pixels.

  47  LS wide                     in   1 table entries, ~0.00% of pixels
      The same least-squares fit as LS, clamped to 2 beyond the range of all 8
      spatial neighbours instead of a, b, c and d. The two differ only where
      the fit lands outside the range of a, b, c and d, typically at steep
      edges and fine stripes where the right value is outside the immediate
      neighbours. Computed from the same fit, so it costs almost nothing
      extra.

(The pixel shares split each average's share equally between its two
primitives and come from BlockWriter's survey below; they add up to 100%, LS
wide being new and so without survey figures. Primitives 10, 12, 14, 15, 17,
20, 21, 22, 26, 27, 33, 38, 41, 43 and 44 appear in no entry of the table;
they stay defined so that old files still decode and future entries can use
them.)

HOW THE TABLE WAS CHOSEN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The old table (PREDICTOR_TABLE_256, used by Block formats 3 to 8 and Delta,
Packet and Simple formats 3 and 4) had 256 entries: 31 primitives alone, 224
averages chosen by measurement on 7 images, and LS. In October 2026
BlockWriter's survey was run on all 19 images of the TestImages repository
(0.2 to 32.7 megapixels) at four settings: Color Resolution 0 / Pixel
Resolution 0 (lossless), CR1/PR1, CR3/PR4 (the writers' default) and CR4/PR5;
76 surveys. 32 entries were never chosen by any channel of any survey; the
other 224, in their old order, are ids 0-223 of the new table, with LS last at
223, followed by LS wide at 224 (Block formats 9 and 10, Delta, Packet and
Simple format 5).

Measured on 7 of the images (ballerina, bluejay, finch, haze, isle3, owl1,
potter) at the four settings, 28 surveys, against the old table: 0.35% smaller
lossless (haze 1.14%, where LS wide covers 87% of the pixels), 0.18% smaller
at CR1/PR1, 0.04% smaller at CR3/PR4 and 0.03% larger at CR4/PR5; 0.21%
smaller in all, no file more than 0.2% larger (ballerina at CR4/PR5, where
every predictor it had chosen is still in the table: the survey's greedy
choice varies a little with the table). Dropping 32 entries makes the survey
roughly 5% faster. Caveat: all 15 JPEGs in TestImages carry ICC profiles, and
the survey harness decoded them with the profile applied, while the writers
strip it; the usage figures for the JPEGs are therefore for slightly different
pixels than the writers see. The two decodings differ by a few levels per
pixel, so the choices should be much the same.
